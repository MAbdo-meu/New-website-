1 نظرة عامة

A plan organised as a journey, not a list

Rather than a catalogue of goals, the 2019/2024 plan follows the results of the previous (2014/2019) cycle and advances through four sequential stages, each answering one question and each broken into concrete steps.

The plan rests on the principle of wise governance and treats quality, internationalism, and governance as the means to excellence. It keeps the vision unchanged from the prior cycle — a serious, committed, and seeking-to-learn university — while reorienting the institution's focus toward maintaining the quality and excellence it has already earned through its Golden Standard accreditation, ISO 9001:2015 certification, and QS three-star rating.

5 Where MEU Stood

The starting point

Stage one of the plan assessed the present through a SWOT analysis and benchmark comparisons against eight universities. A curated view follows.

Strengths

  • Geographical location of the campus
  • Golden Standard quality accreditation
  • International academic & research partnerships
  • Appropriate educational infrastructure
  • Diverse faculty nationalities & backgrounds
  • Green, friendly campus environment

Weaknesses

  • Limited recruitment & alumni tracking
  • High administrative & financial bureaucracy
  • Study plans weighted to theory over practice
  • Weak coordination with labour markets
  • Some inefficient use of resources

Opportunities

  • Political stability in Jordan
  • Demand from Arab & foreign students
  • Rising demand for vocational degrees
  • Growth of distance learning
  • Growth in start-ups & innovation

Threats

  • Community colleges becoming universities
  • Strong inter-university competition
  • Similar programmes across private universities
  • Shifting Ministry laws & regulations
  • Public-university acceptance policies

What these indicator types track +

The full set of 197 indicators is published separately in A Guide to MEU's Key Performance Indicators (National Library decision 4557/9/2019). The plan groups them into three types:

Input indicators — 71 (58 quantitative · 13 qualitative)

Measure the resources and conditions the University puts in: staffing, infrastructure, funding, and environment.

Operational indicators — 58 (56 quantitative · 2 qualitative)

Measure how processes run: the efficiency and quality of teaching, administration, and day-to-day operations.

Output indicators — 68 (51 quantitative · 17 qualitative)

Measure results: graduates, research produced, accreditations earned, and community impact.

Monitoring tools

Tool
Balanced Scorecard
Translates strategy into objectives, targets, and initiatives across four perspectives: financial, customers, internal operations, and learning & growth.
Tool
Benchmark Comparison Cycle
A repeating loop — identify criteria, compose teams, compare, define deviations, rectify — to judge performance against peers and standards.
Tool
RADaR Technique
Results, Approach, Deployment, Assessment & improvement — a structured method for monitoring and measuring performance.
Tool
Continuous Development
Instant detection of deviations, preemptive response, emergency preparedness, and knowledge-sharing to keep improving quality.

Governance & approval +

The plan was outlined by the Strategic Policies Unit and approved by the Council of Deans (Session 04-2020/2021, Decision No. 23, dated 4 October 2020, Rev. b). It is overseen by three bodies:

مجلس الأمناء

Chaired by Dr. Yacoub Adel Nasereddin, with the University President and vice-chairs for financial, international, academic, community-service, and public-relations affairs.

مجلس العمداء

Led by the University President, comprising vice-presidents and the deans of all faculties and deanships.

مجلس الجامعة

A wider body including faculty representatives, department directors, and representatives of the local community, alumni, and students.
