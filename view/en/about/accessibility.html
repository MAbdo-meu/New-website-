Students with Special Needs & Accommodations

Admission & Registration · Deanship of Student Affairs

Every student who is admitted to MEU is entitled to study on equal terms. This page sets out the accommodations available before you arrive, how to request them, and who is responsible for arranging each one. It is maintained jointly by Admission & Registration, the Deanship of Student Affairs and the Deanship of Quality Assurance & Accreditation.

Before you apply

Disclosing a disability never affects an admission decision

Admission is assessed on academic criteria only. Information you share about a disability, chronic condition or learning difference is held by the Deanship of Student Affairs and used solely to arrange support. You may disclose at application, at registration, or at any point during your studies.

Accommodations available

Physical access

Step-free routes between all teaching buildings, accessible parking near each faculty entrance, adapted washrooms, lifts to upper floors and accessible laboratory benches.

Examination arrangements

Extra time, a separate low-distraction room, a reader or scribe, enlarged or high-contrast papers, and permission to use assistive software in timed assessments.

Learning materials

Lecture notes in advance, digital copies compatible with screen readers, recorded lectures where permitted, and enlarged print on request.

Deaf & hard of hearing

Jordanian Sign Language interpretation for lectures and official meetings, note-taking support, and priority seating for lip-reading.

Blind & low vision

Screen-reader workstations in the library, Braille and tactile material on request, orientation walks of the campus before term begins.

Wellbeing & learning support

Confidential counselling, study-skills coaching, and adjusted attendance or deadline arrangements for chronic conditions, agreed with your faculty.

How to request support

  1. Tell us early. Tick the support box on your application form, or email the Deanship of Student Affairs once you have an application number.
  2. Share documentation. A medical or educational-psychology report describing the impact on study — not a diagnosis alone.
  3. Meet your accessibility adviser. A 30-minute meeting, on campus or online, produces a written Student Support Plan.
  4. Plan shared with your faculty. Teaching staff and the Examinations Unit receive only the arrangements, never your medical details.
  5. Reviewed each semester. Arrangements are confirmed or adjusted with you before each examination period.

Website accessibility

This prototype ships an on-page assistive toolbar — the ♿ button at the right of your screen — offering larger text, a high-contrast mode, highlighted links, a more readable typeface and reduced motion. Settings persist as you move through the site. Pages are built to be navigated by keyboard alone, images carry text alternatives in both English and Arabic, and the Arabic interface runs fully right-to-left. Report a barrier and we will respond within five working days.
